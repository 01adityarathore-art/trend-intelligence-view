How we pick the top 5 designs and prints

T-shirts · Lehengas · Tie-dye · India · snapshot 28 Oct 2026

A design makes the top 5 when several independent kinds of evidence agree it is beating its own normal: Google searches growing faster than the category, marketplaces where it sells better than its share of listings, brand sites where it sells out faster than the same store's other pieces, and social posts that get more likes and views than usual. One loud source can't put a design on the list by itself.

2 · The four evidence families

FamilyWhat we compareCounts as upCounts as down
S · Google searchThe design's search growth ÷ the category's growth. Needs 300+ searches/month.≥ 1.08×≤ 0.92×
M · MarketplacesIts share of Amazon "bought" + Myntra/Ajio/Flipkart reviews ÷ its share of listings. Needs 5+ listings.≥ 1.15×≤ 0.87×

Each family needs a minimum sample (300 searches a month, 5 listings, 30 pieces), and social numbers built on few posts are pulled toward 1.0×. A design with 9 pieces can't look like a winner just because 3 of them sold out.

3 · Grades

Confirmed 3+ families up and none down, in two weekly snapshots in a row. Likely 2+ up, none down. Leaning up more up than down. Early signal 1 up, nothing down. Mixed as many up as down. Leaning down, Cooling more down than up. Thin data fewer than 2 families have data.

Top 5 takes designs that are a real slice of the market (at least 1% of design searches, 2% of brand-site pieces or 3% of marketplace listings) and are not falling, ranked by grade, then by how many more families are up than down, then by score (the average of the family numbers). If fewer than 5 qualify, a small design that is clearly rising fills the gap, marked small. This is the first snapshot, so nothing can be Confirmed yet. That needs the next weekly refresh.

Worked example: Henley T-shirts
Search: 278,111/month, +554% vs a year ago, against +37% for all T-shirts → (1 + 5.54) ÷ (1 + 0.37) = 4.77× → up.
Social: likes, views and post share average 2.45× → up. Sell-through: 13 of 84 pieces sold out, 1.02× the stores' normal → flat.
Marketplaces: only 2 listings name Henley, below the 5 needed → no data. Result: 2 up, 0 down → Likely, ranked #1.

4 · T-shirt prints

A T-shirt's design is two things: its style (fit, neck, fabric: oversized, henley, polo…) and its print, meaning what is on it. Prints are mapped in three levels: world (Anime, Superheroes, Gods & spiritual, Sports, Music…), franchise (Naruto, Spider-Man, Mahadev, RCB…) and character (Kakashi, Itachi, Luffy, Gojo…). A character counts towards its franchise and world too. A second dimension, print style, covers how it is printed or placed: back print, puff print, all-over, minimal chest logo, typography.

  • Each print gets the same four families and grades as designs. Only searches that say "t shirt" count, so "spiderman" alone (the film) is not a tee search.
  • Look-alike words are guarded: "Levi" is Levi's jeans unless it says Ackerman or AOT. "Shiv Naresh" and "Duke" are brands, not Mahadev or KTM. "The Bear House" and "American Eagle" are brands, not animals.
  • Culture is a fifth read, kept out of the grade: Google interest in the show, star or team itself, last 13 weeks against the same weeks a year ago. When culture rises and tee searches haven't yet, the print goes on the Next wave list.
  • Words searched with "t shirt" that match no print are listed as spotted, not on our map, so new prints (a new film, a meme) show up even before we name them.

5 · Pattern Studio, Trend Score and Keyword shelves

Pattern Studio shows every print and pattern as Netflix-style rows, highest score first: Top 10, trending prints, trending patterns, rising fast, most popular, best sellers, next wave, characters, one row per world, print styles, and YouTube and Instagram rows. T-shirts cover prints plus 26 patterns (stripes, checks, polka dots, colour-block, ringer & raglan, floral, tropical, animal print, camo, tie-dye, acid wash, ombré, marble, geometric, abstract, paisley, aztec, all-over, digital, plain, waffle & rib, mesh…). Lehengas cover celebrity, designer and film looks, motifs, prints, borders, craft, silhouettes and fabrics. Tie-dye covers its patterns and the garments that carry them.

Trend Score (0–100)
40% how big: searches, pieces on brand sites, marketplace listings and Amazon bought, as a percentile among its peers
+ 40% how fast it is growing: search growth vs the whole category, social lift, culture
+ 20% how many sources agree: evidence families up minus down
Example: Ringer & raglan scores 93 (big 89 · growing 93 · agree 100): 22K searches a month (+197%), Amazon 2.7× its share, 52 of 176 sold out.

A print is compared like with like. Its marketplace pull is measured against other print listings, not against Amazon's best-selling plain basics.

Keyword shelves list the 100 biggest Google searches that name a print or pattern (at most 4 per print, spelling variants counted once). Under each one sit the products Google Shopping shows for that exact search, Google's own filter chips, the stores that win the shelf, and the price range. Myntra, Ajio and Flipkart appear only as Google shows them.

6 · Top 5 Designs and the Buyer Intent Score

Top 5 Designs turns the data into five products to make in each category, where buyer intent is highest. Each brief gives the garment, print or pattern, fit, fabric, colourways, price to win, audience, occasion, the Google search to target, a listing title, the channel, why it will sell, a model casting with the reason, and the main risk.

Buyer Intent Score (0–100)
30% buying searches: searches with price, buy, online, under ₹, a marketplace or brand name, or Google's own transactional label
+ 15% ad price per click on those searches (advertisers pay more where people buy)
+ 30% proof of purchase: Amazon "bought in past month", brand-site sell-outs, Myntra/Ajio/Flipkart review counts
+ 15% growth + 10% sources agreeing (from the Trend Score). Percentiles within the category.
  • Licence first. Character, team, player, celebrity and designer names score high but need a licence. Amazon and Myntra take unlicensed tees down. They are listed separately, never in the five.
  • Prices to win come from where purchases cluster: Amazon's bought-weighted price, brand-site sell-out medians, and the Google Shopping shelf, cleaned of accessories (₹179 latkans aren't lehengas).
  • Model casting follows the buyer: gender from searches that name one, life stage from searches and from how pieces are labelled, styling from the occasion. Model photos are AI images (Z-Image Turbo, Apache-2.0, on the free Kaggle T4) of people who don't exist. The design sheets are vector flats Claude draws from each brief, exact to the spec, with clickable colourways.
  • Two overrides, both stated on the page: lehenga #5 is Banarasi (the biggest buy-search) over Chikankari, whose sell-outs sit in stores where most pieces sell out; tie-dye #5 is acid wash (56,790 buy-searches) over Pastel (7 pieces, no sales proof).

7 · Life stages

"Who it is for" is read from what people type and how pieces are sold: words like baby, kids, boys, girls, teen, college, bride, bridesmaid, mom; sizes (2–3Y, 9–10Y); and style cues (anime, Korean and aesthetic lean teens; oversized, gym and couple lean young adults; polo, office and plus size lean adults). It is a reading of words, not demographic data.

8 · Clean-ups we applied

  • One-month spikes. Google's data showed "lehenga" at 20.4M searches in Sep 2025 against a usual 0.4M. Months above 8× a query's median are set to its highest normal month. Without this fix, every lehenga design looked like it was rising.
  • Variants. Google gives close variants the same monthly series. At 20K+ searches, an identical moving series plus a shared word is counted once ("r k tshirt" is the same people as "tshirt").
  • Noise. Skincare ("salicylic acid wash"), hairstyles for lehengas, jeans and home-dye kits are removed or moved to their own group.
  • Tie-dye patterns. Titles rarely name the pattern, so Rainbow, Pastel and Monochrome are also read from the colours a title names when it names no pattern.

Limits

  • The print-specific YouTube pass (Naruto tee hauls, anime merch) needs a fresh day of the free YouTube quota. Until it runs, video signals come from the 734 T-shirt videos already collected.
  • One snapshot shows where things stand now. Two weekly snapshots turn Likely into Confirmed.
  • Tie-dye has the thinnest evidence. Most listings say "tie dye" without the pattern, so its grades are mostly Mixed.
  • Some lehenga designs (Chikankari, Kalamkari) have little search data under those names. Their grades lean on sell-through and social.
  • Brand sites are Shopify stores that publish their catalogue. Myntra, Ajio, Flipkart and Meesho are read only through Google Shopping.
